IWA Headquarters

We toured the IWA headquarters facilities including the sports hall, gym, and community centre. Then we sat down with Ann, a receptionist at IWA for the past 15 years, to discuss her experiences as a wheelchair user and community member.

What is the IWA?

The IWA, Irish Wheelchair Association, is Ireland's leading organization for people with physical disabilities. They serve over 20,000 members across the nation through 58 different centres.

The IWA was founded in the 1960s by eight Paralympians. During and prior to that time, people with disabilites in Ireland lived in hospitals, were institutionalized, or never left their houses. IWA offered tehm a cahnce to get out and try sports. Then, as IWA grew, they continued to offer an increasing number of services to the community. They now offer a variety of services including assisted living, community centre, disabled parking permits, sports, and more.

You can learn more at the IWA website.

IWA Headquarters

Facilities

Sports Hall
Basketball court with wide pathways on all sides to allow wheelchair users, both spectators and players, to easily navigate the space even while there is a game happening the court. The sports hall is also used to play Boccia, a paralympic sport similar to bocce ball.
Gym
The gym is open to disabled and non-disabled members. It features accessible equipment such as a wider bench for lifting, ergs with removable seats, and weight machines with removable chairs so wheelchair users can roll right up to the machine to use it. Additionally, the gym has hand bikes, ski ergs, and a studio where the IWA holds adaptive fitness classes.
Community Centre
One of 58 community centres nationwide. It features a day program with daily activities such as arts and crafts, bingo, and baking. Many members attend the day programs five days a week. There is a cafeteria called the Wheelzcafe which serves breakfast and lunch to members and staff daily.

Speaking with Ann

Ann is a power wheelchair user. She is a receptionist at IWA where she has worked for the past 15 years. She is originally from Kilkenny, and has lived in Dublin for the past 31 years.

Ann spoke to us about her experiences as a wheelchair user in Dublin. She also spoke briefly about her upbringing including living in residential care from two to eleven years old. A main focus of our conversation was on issues surrounding transportation for wheelchair users.

It can be a daily struggle especially for people in rural areas. Ann mentioned that she is frustrated that people will park directly in front of cub cuts at crosswalks since this makes it so she cannot cross the street at that location. This is a frequent occurrence.As Ann explained disability often leads to additional costs both when it comes to transportation and more generally. Disabled individuals often have to pay more to get access to forms of transport that are accessible to them such as takng an accessible taxi when able-bodied people could just take any old bus. Unfortunately, the IWA is not able to provide financial assistance to cover these increased transporation costs.

The IWA is able to issue parking permits to park in disabled parking bays. The permits are valid in Ireland and abroad. Ireland also recognizes disabled parking permits from other countries as well.

Memorable Quotes from Ann

  • "My powerchair is my legs."
  • "If you can help somebody, it's the right thing to do."
  • “When it’s Friday, it’s a nightmare.”
    • Friday is bin day which means that there are often bins blocking the sidewalks making it very challenging to get around Dublin in your wheelchair on Fridays especially.

Class Reflections

What was your favorite part of our tour today?

Overwhelmingly, our favorite part of the tour was getting to speak with Ann about her experiences. As one person put it, "She offered a lot of insight and real world knowledge." Another said, "My favorite part of our tour was hearing from Anne. For one, it was exciting to hear from someone about life in Dublin. It was also interesting but saddening to hear about the struggles with accessibility that she’s faced."

What was one idea that stuck with you?
  • The amount of extra time, effort, and money that disabled people have to expend in order to travel, socialize, and go to work. I think this reinforces our role as web designers to follow the principles of POUR and create good experiences for all users.
  • How much planning has to go into every outing for someone in a wheelchair
  • The public transportation restrictions and the limitations able bodied people don’t notice
  • It stuck with me that the youngest person to be in the sports program was 5 years old, I think that is so cool that it is an option for younger children to be involved in sports. And it’s important for them to see that there are many options at a young age for people with disabilities.
What do you remember about the spaces we toured?
  • The sports hall had wide pathways around the whole basketball court
  • All of the bathrooms were accessible and the doors were light and had a second handle to help wheelchairs users pull them closed independently
  • A lot of the facilities were used by both wheelchair and non-wheelchair users. I had not seen moveable bleachers before and noticed the bench press had the biggest bench I have seen in a gym
  • There was a lot of natural lighting in the game room and around the center with calming colors like green
  • The gym having accessibility features was really cool
  • The bench in the gym was slightly wider than normal and many stations had step tools for when wheelchair users need to transfer onto the seats safely.
