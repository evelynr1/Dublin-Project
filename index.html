Céad míle fáilte!

One hundred thousand welcomes! My name is Evelyn Russell and I have spent three weeks studying web accessibility in Dublin, Ireland. I have spent my time exploring the city, along with a few other adventures including the cliff walk in Howth and a bus tour to Wickow, Glendalough, and Kilkenny.

Through my time living and learning here, I have focused on accessibility in both physical and digital spaces. Specifically, I have wandered through many Dublin parks looking for how the physical design of the spaces enables and invites interaction from the public. Which parks have the most comfortable benches, the most beautiful views, the coolest water features? And also which parks are accessible for wheelchair users? Which have clear signage? Which parks have playgrounds or spaces for those who need to move their bodies in order to relax?

This site is the incomplete culmination of my observations. Of all of the parks I visited, I picked five to spotlight.

  • Dubh Linn Gardens
  • Iveagh Gardens
  • Peace Park
  • St Patrick's Park
  • St Stephen's Greens

These five are centrally located in Dublin which means they are accessible by public transit. Other honorable mentions include Phoenix Park (the biggest park in Dublin) and the Dublin Zoo. While the Dublin Zoo is not a park in the traditional sense, it is a fantastic place to spend an afternoon learning about the conservation work being done in Dublin in collaboration with zoos around the world.
