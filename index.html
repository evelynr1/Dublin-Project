Parks in Dublin

Céad míle fáilte!

One hundred thousand welcomes! My name is Evelyn Russell and I have spent three weeks studying web accessibility in Dublin, Ireland. Much of my time here has been spent enjoying the numerous parks and greenspaces the city has to offer. Of all of the parks I visited, I have picked five to spotlight!

  • Dubh Linn
  • Iveagh Gardens
  • The Peace Park
  • St Patrick's Park
  • St Stephen's Green

These five are centrally located in Dublin which means they are accessible by public transit. Other honorable mentions include Phoenix Park (the biggest park in Dublin and largest enclosed park in Europe) and the Dublin Zoo. While the Dublin Zoo is not a park in the traditional sense, it is a fantastic place to spend an afternoon learning about the conservation work being done in Dublin in collaboration with zoos around the world.

Why Dublin?

I have been in Dublin taking a three week course on web accessibility with University of Michigan School of Information Professor Colleen Van Lent. Through this course, I have learned about both accessibility and Irish culture. It gave me a new perspective on how I conceptualize what it means for a place to be accessible without being limited by the American understanding of the term.

Why Parks?

Through my time living and learning here, I have focused on accessibility in both physical and digital spaces. Specifically, I have wandered through many Dublin parks looking for how the physical design of the spaces enables and invites interaction from the public.

Which parks have the most comfortable benches? Which parks are accessible for wheelchair users? Which parks have playgrounds or spaces for those who need to move their bodies in order to relax?
